# Pabst and Riverside theater exteriors

Both models share production geometry with their review pages, and appear in the district landmark menu, tour and walking destinations. Day, sunset and night presets update windows and sign lighting. This work models the public exteriors; optional auditorium reference photographs were not used to create navigable interiors.

## Pabst Theater

The [NPS nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/pdfhost/docs/NHLS/Text/72000063.pdf) establishes the south-facing Wells Street frontage and roughly 148 × 80 ft historic body. It distinguishes the iron porch, east entrance pavilion, central balcony, blind arcade bays and taller west stage roof. These control the model’s composition.

The supplied broad facade photograph guides ornament, proportions and current blade cabinets. Its second exterior file depicts plaques, despite its filename. The mapped way 68649538 includes a larger envelope than the documented body: the historic body keeps its proportions and the porch/annex are subordinate additions. Their precise separation and rear details remain inferred because the cached map has no building parts.

Pabst sign faces use separate outward-reading geometry with layered dark/gold cabinets and original lettering; neither side is a mirrored copy of the other. The review has camera presets for both faces.

## Riverside Theater / Empire Building

The full mapped Empire Building (way 68641417) replaces the generic extrusion. Its diagonal riverside footprint is preserved; the Wisconsin Avenue frontage is a separate near east–west plane. The canopy projection is omitted from the tower outline and modeled at entrance height. A second cached roof object (way 397489770) was incorrectly extruded as a solid block by the base map; its exact placeholder signature is also removed so it cannot obscure the entrance. The entrance has a true recessed door bank, brass hardware, transoms, ticket windows, poster cases and canopy coffers. Its finished floor follows the cached Wisconsin Avenue road frontage, with a solid sloped apron tagged for pedestrian movement. This avoids burying the doorway at the lower terrain elevation beneath the building center.

[Milwaukee Public Library’s 2015 account](https://www.mpl.org/blog/now/riverside-unvelis-new-marquee-and-blade) confirms the red 40-foot blade. Supplied photos guide the canopy, side returns, circular sign backing, ornament and underside bulb rows. Blade mounting elevation, canopy dimensions and window spacing are photo estimates. Tower height uses the cached building data; published floor counts differ, and this is not a measured architectural survey.

## Data and checks

Cached source: September 6, 2026 Geofabrik/OSM extract. Coordinate axes are X east and Z south. Source-node and height matching removes only the old theater/Empire triangles at both tile detail levels, preserving neighboring geometry and streets.

The reference photos remain outside the repository. Masonry, ornaments, lettering, signs and lamps are generated geometry; actual event posters and advertising content are not reproduced.

Checks cover roof winding, material merging, finite geometry, mapped placement, foundation contact, sign direction and lighting resets. Browser comparison, including pedestrian views, supplements the code checks; passing tests alone does not establish exact visual accuracy.
