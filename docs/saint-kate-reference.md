# Saint Kate — The Arts Hotel

The hotel is a district landmark, tour stop and walking destination. `saint-kate-review.html` uses the same geometry as the city and loads the shipped terrain elevations for ground-contact review. Day, sunset and night modes control window, canopy and red sign lighting.

## Visual references

The supplied reference pack includes several mislabeled images. `sk_11` is the exterior aerial with the east window grid and south corner neon. `sk_12` is the close view of the north entrance with its ochre fascia, three glazed door groups and patterned canopy fixtures. `sk_13` shows the river-side terrace of the adjacent complex, while `sk_14` is a lobby photograph. Model decisions use visible content rather than filenames.

[The hotel’s official site](https://www.saintkatearts.com/) establishes its Kilbourn Avenue address. [Stonehill Taylor’s project](https://stonehilltaylor.com/project/saint-kate/) supplies the entry and public-space design references. The model uses original geometry and lettering; no photographs or displayed artwork are shipped as textures. Lobby detail is limited to what reads through the public entrance, rather than a navigable interior reconstruction.

## Placement and scope

OSM way 69020648 defines the hotel footprint independently of the surrounding Milwaukee Center podium, Associated Bank River Center and theater. The north-facing entrance projection is present in that ring. Exact source-node and height matching removes only the former hotel extrusion at both tile detail levels.

The [Upper Bar reference](https://www.saintkatearts.com/dining/upper-bar) is riverfront context for the neighboring complex. Its curved terrace is not attached to the hotel roof in this model. The neighboring source geometry remains intact.

Guest-room window spacing, arch profiles, ornaments and light levels are photo-based approximations. Tests check geometry, sign direction, lighting resets, mapped replacement and street-level ground contact; browser inspection checks the visual result. This is an interpretive exterior, not a measured architectural survey.
